1. SUMMARY OF THE RESULTS
====================================================================================================

 - Unit tests: all 37 tests in the chess project pass (26 index/drill/proof tests, 7 Lists 1-2 tests, 4 move
   generation tests). 12 seconds including the build.
 - King and pawn against king (KPK) is SOLVED. 1,400,000 drill games (self-play) classified all 165,676 distinct
   KPK positions: 111,282 are wins for the pawn side, 54,394 are draws, 0 are unproven.
 - That result was checked against a separate, plain retrograde solution of the endgame: every proven win and every
   proven draw agrees (0 wrong). The program's own tests also contain this check at a smaller size.
 - Longest forced win in KPK: 54 individual moves (plies), which is 27 full moves. The program's "win in N" counts
   individual moves (plies): "win in 13" means 7 full moves. N is the distance of the line the program found, not
   always the shortest win: 80,595 of the 111,282 wins have a longer stored distance than the shortest possible.
 - Replaying 1,989 proven wins spread over the whole endgame: all 1,989 ended in promotion after exactly the stored
   number of plies (0 off by even one).
 - The 20 strategies: all 20 were found in the archives. KPK drill games (19.8 million archived moves) show 13 of
   the 20; 8 famous full games add the rest. With the flags the archive stores (the mover's), 19 of 20 reach the
   COMPLETION stage; the 20th (Opposite-Side Castling Attack) completes only on the other side's flags in one game.
 - Lists 3 and 4 work as the rules say: from an empty archive every move is "never tried"; from 8 stored games the
   starting position already ranks e4 (played 5 times, 5 wins) ahead of d4, Nf3 and f3; List 4 shows only exchanges
   that really happened (for example Bxf3 Qxf3 dxe5: gain 420, loss 330, net 90).
 - Algebra file: 1,000 problems, 55 categories, 327 distinct wordings (numbers masked). A simple word-matching
   test finds the right category 99% of the time when the same wording is in the archive, but only about 50% of the
   time for a brand-new wording (equation shape: 92% and 22%). Match confidence is informative: the most confident
   quarter of matches were right about 93% of the time on known types.
 - Conclusion: the chess program's design (normalized key + archive index + proof by an independent check + four
   lists) transfers to the algebra operator. Its code does not, and the matching accuracy for new wordings must
   come from good cue features and a growing archive, not from the chess ideas alone. See part 7.

6. THE ALGEBRA WORD PROBLEM FILE AND A MATCHING EXPERIMENT
====================================================================================================

Files in the project folder (written by Claude, not yet in the repository):
  algebra_word_problems.txt                    1,000 problems with worked solutions
  algebra_word_problems_note_from_claude.txt   a description of that file

The file
  - Entry layout: "#0001 [Category]" / Problem / Let / Equation / Solve / Answer, entries separated by a line of dashes.
  - 1,000 problems, 55 categories, 216 problem types (as stated in the note), at most 5 problems per type.
  - 22.9 words per problem on average (6 to 51), 3.17 numbers per problem on average (1 to 10).
  - Masking the numbers gives 479 distinct sentences; masking names too gives 327 distinct wordings and 180 distinct
    equation shapes.
  - The note says all 1,000 answers were checked by substituting them back into their equations.

The experiment (algebra_match_experiment.py): how well does plain word matching find the type of a problem?
  Method: numbers -> #, names -> <name>, drop common words, one-word and two-word features, TF-IDF cosine nearest
  neighbour. "Leave one wording out" removes every problem with the same wording before the match is made, so the
  matcher must handle a wording it has never stored.

  problems: 1000  categories: 55  distinct wordings: 327  distinct equation shapes: 180
  
  A) same wording may be in the archive (leave one out)
     category right: 99.1%   equation shape right: 92.0%
  
  B) the exact wording is NOT in the archive (leave one wording out)
     category right (best match): 50.4%   in the best 3: 52.2%
     equation shape right (best match): 22.3%   in the best 3: 23.8%
     most confident  25% of the matches: category right 85.6%, shape right 53.2%  (score >= 0.57)
     most confident  50% of the matches: category right 69.8%, shape right 32.2%  (score >= 0.35)
     most confident  75% of the matches: category right 54.7%, shape right 23.9%  (score >= 0.22)
     most confident 100% of the matches: category right 50.4%, shape right 22.3%  (score >= 0.02)
  
  C) new wording + 15%% of the words dropped + different numbers
     category right: 49.0%   equation shape right: 21.6%
  
  D) most common wrong category matches under B (true -> matched):
     Linear cost                  -> Break-even                   28 of 60
     Number puzzle                -> Percent                      11 of 42
     Geometry (quadratic)         -> Geometry (area)              10 of 32
     Linear cost                  -> Direct variation             10 of 60
     Linear model                 -> Formula                      10 of 52
     Rational equation            -> Current / wind               10 of 36
     Geometry (area)              -> Geometry (quadratic)         9 of 18
     System of equations          -> Proportion                   9 of 68
  
  numbers per problem: avg 3.08; problems with >=5 numbers: 74
  
  B2) leave one wording out, split by whether the archive still holds that problem TYPE (category + equation shape)

  - In this file 545 of the 1,000 problems belong to a type that has only ONE wording, so "a type the archive has

7. WHAT I LEARNED, AND HOW THE ARCHIVE AND THE FOUR LISTS COULD PICK THE ALGEBRA PROBLEM
====================================================================================================

7.1 What the chess program does (facts from the code, the rules and the tests)
  - rules/rules.txt is the specification and the README explains the behavior. Nothing is looked ahead.
  - List 1 and List 2 use only the present game: the last five turns and the 20 pre-made templates. A strategy is
    listed when its foundation, progress or completion is met (scores 30 / 60 / 100). List 2 prices the captures
    those strategies allow, net of the cost of losing the capturing piece.
  - List 3 and List 4 use only past games, through a position index: one entry per distinct position, with the
    legal moves, how often each was played, the wins/draws/losses, and per strategy the stage pairs one game
    produced. List 3 has one row per legal move: proven win in N, proven draw, tried before (with counts), or never
    tried. List 4 shows the captures and promotions past games really made after that move.
  - A position key is normalized (color flipped, board mirrored) so equivalent positions share one entry: 331,352
    KPK positions become 165,676.
  - "Proven" comes from bookkeeping, not from counts: a backward update through the stored moves plus a closing rule
    for draws. The program never calls a position proven because it won often. The independent retrograde solution
    confirmed that 0 of 165,676 proofs are wrong.
  - Similar positions are only used for KPK, are labeled "similar", and are never proof. In ordinary middlegames the
    lists show only exact past positions.
  - The drill plays both sides, tries never-tried moves first, and needs about 1.3 million games to prove the whole
    endgame. Positions are all seen far earlier than they are proven.
  - The archive saves 20 strategy flags for every move, so the archive is also a record of which strategies occurred.
  - Weakness found: the stored "win in N" is not always the shortest win (72% of KPK wins have a longer stored
    distance than the shortest). The win/draw answer is exact; the distance is a bound.

7.2 How each piece maps onto algebra word problems (a proposal, not built)
  Chess program                          Algebra word problem operator
  ------------------------------------   ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  position                               the user's text
  the five-turn window                   the sentences read so far
  normalized position key                normalized wording: numbers -> #, names -> <name>, units and words
                                         standardized. One archive entry per distinct normalized wording
                                         (the file has 327, not 1,000)
  20 strategy templates                  about 20 problem families (age, percent, motion, work, mixture, geometry,
                                         number puzzles, ratio, interest, break-even, systems, inequality ...), each
                                         with foundation (cue words present), progress (the needed kinds of
                                         quantities present) and completion (every box can be filled, the question
                                         is recognised). The file's 55 categories are the starting point.
  List 1 (present game only)             families forming in this text, scored 30 / 60 / 100
  List 2 (captures, net value)           which number goes in which box: candidate assignments scored by gain
                                         (unit and nearby words agree) minus the cost of a wrong assignment
  List 3 (archive lookup per move)       one row per candidate family/equation: proven (a stored match whose answer
                                         passed the substitution check and was confirmed), tried (counts: confirmed,
                                         corrected, rejected), never tried; a "similar" row for a new wording, never
                                         shown as proof
  List 4 (what really happened next)     what past users really changed after that match (picked another family,
                                         swapped two numbers), not a guess
  proof / retrograde check               solve the equation and substitute the answer back. The problem file already
                                         has this for all 1,000 answers; no answer is "proven" without it
  drill (self-play)                      generate problems from the 216 types, try every candidate family, keep what
                                         verifies, so the archive grows before any user types anything
  banks (general / drill)                a bank for the 1,000 supplied problems and a bank for matches the user
                                         confirmed, so counts can be read separately

  Suggested flow when text is pasted from the template box:
    1. Split the text deterministically into word boxes and number boxes (numbers with their units/percent/dollar
       signs). This needs no archive and no guessing, and is what the row will show.
    2. Normalize it and look it up in the archive. An exact wording gives an answer straight away (99% / 92% in the
       experiment above).
    3. If not found, build List 1 from cue features and List 3 "similar" rows from the nearest stored wording.
       Show the best match only when its score is high (0.70 and up was right about 93% of the time on known
       types); otherwise show the best 3 and ask the user, as the chess program asks again on a bad promotion answer.
    4. Fill the boxes using the matched problem's equation, solve it, substitute back. Only a verified match is
       stored as proven; the user's confirm / correct is what feeds counts and List 4.

7.3 What the chess ideas do NOT give us
  - Matching accuracy. Chess positions repeat exactly (165,676 KPK positions, all seen within 200,000 games). Word
    problems do not; rules.txt itself says that where positions rarely repeat, the lists show only exact past
    positions and nothing is proven. The weak spot is the step from a new wording to the right family, and the
    experiment shows plain word similarity is not enough there (about 50% category for a new wording).
  - A closed space. KPK could be solved because it is finite. Language is not, so the archive can only grow in
    coverage; it can never be "solved".
  - Reusable code. The program is Rust and built around chess positions. What transfers is the design, and I would
    write the algebra version in Python inside the calculator.
  - The numbers inside the equation. Some equation numbers are derived from the problem's numbers (for example 55%
    boys gives 0.45n, 10% up then 25% down gives 1.1 x 0.75). The file gives finished equations but not the
    generator that built them, so each type needs its own small formula, or a symbolic solver, to take a user's new
    numbers to a correct equation.

7.4 Decisions for you before I build anything
  1. Scope of a first version. My suggestion: (a) the deterministic text/number box split, (b) exact-wording
     lookup plus nearest-wording "similar" rows with a confidence threshold, (c) fill the boxes and solve by
     substituting into the matched problem's equation, with substitution check, for the types where that works.
  2. The 20 families. Do you want families fixed by hand from the 55 categories, or learned from the file?
  3. Where the archive lives: a file like saved_templates.json for the user's confirmed matches, plus the 1,000
     supplied problems as the starting bank.
  4. Whether the two text files (and this chess_experiments folder) should go into the repository.
