WHAT ALREADY EXISTS  (you saw part of it with the Navier-Stokes rows)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Every card of a calculator row has two boxes for words: Label (called Item on a number card) and Context.
     - Python: real text boxes beside each card (before this they were dead buttons).  The Descriptive Columns Console buttons and "Apply Word" put a word
       into the box that was last clicked.
     - Rust: a "Label / Context boxes" checkbox above the rows shows them under every card; it turns on by itself for an operator that comes with words.
       Hover a box to read all of it.
     - What you type replaces the operator's words for that row; when the row's math changes, typed words are forgotten.
2. An operator can come WITH words.  Where they are written, one source for both programs:
     operator_keys/operator_row_specs.txt   one line per operator, in the language described at the top of operator_keys/make_operator_row_files.py.
         write  symbol{label|context}  right after a symbol, e.g.   ρ{density|mass of fluid in each unit of volume (kg/m³)}
         write  {sub:y|label|context}  for a letter one grid row down.        Spaces inside the braces are fine; braces and | are not.
     python operator_keys/make_operator_row_files.py     writes  WORDS = {card index: (label, context)}  into operator_row_math/<operator>.py
     python operator_keys/make_rust_layouts.py           copies WORDS into rust_calculator/src/layouts.rs  (do not edit those two by hand)
   The five Navier-Stokes rows are the worked example: look at their lines in operator_row_specs.txt.
3. Several equations on several rows: operator_row_math/multirow_specs.py (Python) and rust_calculator/src/multirow.rs (Rust, hand-written, same text).
   Each row of a multi-row operator is an OPERATOR of its own (its own folder, row file and picture).  Rows are joined by a math sign.
   Navier-Stokes is the example: navier_stokes_equation (vector form) + x/y/z momentum + continuity = five rows.
4. Pictures: operators_one_operator_per_file/<group>/<name>/<name>.jpg.  The Navier-Stokes ones are drawn FROM the cards of their rows by
   operator_keys/make_navier_stokes_pictures.py (one glyph per card, in its own column and grid row, using the same planner as the calculator).
   200 other operator pictures were redrawn by operator_keys/make_operator_pictures.py; 20 are your drawings.

Design rules that must stay true (they are in README.md, "Design rules for the rows"):
   - Math that can be written on several lines is written on several lines: fraction = numerator row, bar, denominator row; exponent one row up.
   - The lines of one equation are ONE calculator row, not Row 2, Row 3.
   - The row follows its picture, and the picture follows the row.  They must agree.
   - Python and Rust must agree.  Files marked GENERATED are never edited by hand.
